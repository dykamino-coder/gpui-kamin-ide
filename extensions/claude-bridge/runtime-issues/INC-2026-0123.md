# INC-2026-0123 — CefShutdown takes tens of seconds on exit when the machine is under full CPU load

- **Status:** investigation. The symptom is measured and a stack is captured, but the waiting point inside CEF is not yet named.
- **Registration author:** `kacc10`, Bridge executor, lead-directed (2026-11-16). Found during the runtime gate of [#999](https://github.com/dykamino-coder/gpui-kamin-ide/pull/999) (INC-2026-0122).
- **Completion target:** the cause of the slow `CefShutdown`, proven by a stack with CEF symbols, then a fix. After the window closes, the instance must exit within 5 s even under load, without damaging the CEF profile.
- **Diagnostic PR:** [#1000](https://github.com/dykamino-coder/gpui-kamin-ide/pull/1000)
- **Fix PR:** none
- **Next step:** investigation, owned by `kacc10`.
  - Fetch the CEF release symbols that match the bundled `libcef.dll`.
  - Take stacks of the browser process and of every child process during a slow `CefShutdown`.
  - Name the wait. A forced-exit watchdog is not acceptable until the stack shows that it cannot damage the profile.
- **Private evidence:** not published. The captured stacks and run logs stay in the executor's local diagnostics folder; the card states only thread names, wait functions and timings.
- **Dependencies:** [#999](https://github.com/dykamino-coder/gpui-kamin-ide/pull/999). That PR adds the `[exit]` phase lines in `diag.log` used below, and it closes the browsers before `CefShutdown`.
- **Acceptance:** a Windows runtime run on the fix head. At least 10 closes run while another build saturates the CPU. Every one must exit within 5 s, and the next start must open without Chromium's profile dialog.
- **Acceptance rationale:** the delay exists only in real CEF processes under real load, so a unit test cannot show it.
- **Deployment observation owner:** none. The local KaminIDE installation is the acceptance stand.

## Symptom

KaminIDE with the #999 fix sometimes stays alive for 9 to 65 s after its window closes. The window loop has already ended and all browsers are closed; the time is spent inside `cef::shutdown()`.

## Confirmed facts

- **Measurement.** The #999 gate ran 60 close cycles of a separate copy. `diag.log` records `[exit] window loop finished`, `[exit] browsers closed in N ms` and `[exit] CEF stopped in N ms`.
  - With average CPU under 80%, 51 of 53 cycles exited within 3 s, and `CefShutdown` usually took 0.1 to 0.8 s. The two exceptions took 2.8 s and 38 s; the 38 s one ran right after a debugger had been attached to the previous instance.
  - With CPU at 80–100% from other compilers on the machine, 6 of 7 cycles took 9 to 65 s. `CefShutdown` alone took 8.9, 13.5, 15.7, 27.2, 48.1 and 65.1 s.
  - An earlier build of the same fix showed one 47.6 s case.
- **Browsers are not the cause.** In every slow cycle, all 4 browsers had reported `on_before_close` within 16–47 ms before `CefShutdown` started.
- **Stack.** A non-invasive stack of one hung browser process (exports only, no CEF symbols) was taken 15 s after close.
  - The main thread is in `cef::shutdown`, waiting in `WaitForSingleObjectEx`.
  - The thread `CrBrowserMain` is also waiting on an event.
  - All 64 threads are in wait functions: 30 in `NtWaitForSingleObject`, 9 on worker factories, 8 on alerts and 6 in message waits. No thread is in file I/O.
  - The child processes, renderer and GPU, were still alive at that point.
- **Profile.** After all 60 cycles, including the slow ones, the next start showed no Chromium profile dialog.
- **Additional measurement on the published release (2026-11-19, `teamops29`).** It was taken during the Windows runtime gate of #1043, 16 closes in alternation.
  - Builds measured:
    - 8 closes of the published KaminIDE 1.0.116 (`a52709b9`), which already contains the #999 fix (`b5778a11` is an ancestor).
    - 8 closes of a candidate build of #1043 (`3f0a54d8`).
  - Load: engine benchmark builds (`CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=4`) and benchmark sweeps ran on the same machine, so this is the loaded case. CPU percentage was not recorded.
  - Method: WM_CLOSE to the main window of a separate copy with its own `LOCALAPPDATA`. The time is measured until the root process and all 6 of its children have exited, with a 30 s limit.
  - In every close the window disappeared within 70–526 ms.
  - 1.0.116: 6 of 8 exited, in 486, 576, 1224, 1274, 5249 and 13437 ms. The other 2 still had 2 processes (the root and one child) alive at 30 s.
  - #1043 candidate: 6 of 8 exited, in 425, 534, 590, 1275, 4920 and 7744 ms. The other 2 still had 2 and 3 processes alive at 30 s.
  - Every process left at 30 s had exited later on its own; none had to be killed.
  - Both builds show the same distribution, so the slow exit is not introduced by #1043.
  - 7 of 16 closes missed the 5 s target: 4 did not finish within 30 s and 3 took 5.2–13.4 s. Under load the defect therefore still reproduces on the published release that contains #999.
  - The `[exit]` phase lines of `diag.log` were not read for these runs. The split between closing the browsers and `CefShutdown` is not established for them.

## Hypotheses

- The browser process waits for its child processes to exit, and the children are starved under full CPU load.
- Thread-pool tasks marked `BLOCK_SHUTDOWN` wait behind CPU contention.

Neither is shown yet: the stack has no CEF symbols, and no child stacks were taken.

## Expected outcome

Exit within 5 s under load, with the profile intact.

## Reproduction

1. Saturate the CPU, for example with a multi-job `cargo build`.
2. Start KaminIDE built from #999 and wait for the main window.
3. Close it with WM_CLOSE or the title bar button.
4. Read the `[exit]` lines for that process id in `diag.log`. `CEF stopped in` is several seconds or more.
