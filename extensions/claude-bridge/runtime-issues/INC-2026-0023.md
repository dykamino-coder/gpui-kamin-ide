## Symptom

After successful Direct3D device recreation, rendering an existing scene with
groups, blur or blend effects can reuse device-bound resources from the previous
device. This is separate from failure/retry ownership hazards in INC-2026-0020
and from CEF interop in INC-2026-0022. Its role in the reported sleep/RDP crash
and the driver's response to those calls remain unknown.

## Confirmed facts

Source: `a34ab4892058bcacc00de8d97ae951fb60551780`.
Renderer anchors below refer to
`vendor/gpui/src/platform/windows/directx_renderer.rs`.

- Device-bound fields include `blur` and the lazy blend states at lines 49–61.
  Successful `handle_device_lost_impl` replaces devices/resources/globals/
  pipelines/DirectComposition at 498–504 but leaves those extra caches intact.
- `render_groups` at 617–631 reuses existing group textures if their count and
  dimensions suffice. It then submits their old RTV to the new context at
  637–646. An effects scene with unchanged dimensions exposes this branch.
- `ensure_blur_globals` at 846–860 returns immediately when a buffer already
  exists, without checking its device. The lazy blend getters at 515–528 also
  reuse existing states based only on presence.
- The blur copy cache at 1214–1228 and 1338–1351 checks dimensions, not device
  generation, before `CopyResource` with the new device's back buffer/context.
- The atlas is a counterexample: `directx_atlas.rs:155–168` replaces its device
  references and clears texture caches. Resetting the atlas does not reset the
  renderer's separate effect resources. Scenes without these effects avoid
  the affected branches; size changes refresh some textures but not all states.

Two source reviews checked construction, replacement and reuse. No Windows
execution or measured crash was obtained. Microsoft's
[device-loss recovery guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/uwp/gaming/handling-device-lost-scenarios)
requires recreation of device-dependent resources. The confirmed result here is
stale resource submission, not a demonstrated native failure or measured GPU load.

## Expected outcome

Invalidate or replace every device-bound effect cache when installing a new
device. Include group targets and partially constructed effects in the ownership
review. Existing dimensions and an occupied cache must not authorize use across
device generations.

Automated coverage populates group/blur/blend caches on generation A, completes
recovery to B with unchanged dimensions, and asserts that every submitted
resource belongs to B. Also cover changed dimensions, no-effects scenes and
repeated recovery. A disposable Windows gate renders populated effects before
and after sleep, RDP transitions and controlled device recreation, checks D3D
diagnostics and verifies visuals/input and release of old resources. Those
gates remain pending; this Diagnostic PR contains no functional fix.

## Windows recovery acceptance (2026-10-11, partial)

Run on main `22fb1ad` (debug build with the #216 fix and the #232
application-level seams) on a Windows 11 console session (Intel Iris Xe):
GPUI's device was an application-owned D3D11On12 device and
`nativeAcceptance deviceLoss` called `ID3D12Device5::RemoveDevice`, a genuine
device removal without any system setting. Five removals in a row on the
native UI (no CEF view): before each, a toast with `paint_backdrop_blur`
populated the effect caches; removals 1–2 kept the window size, 3–5 resized
it between recoveries. Result: 5/5 `device_changed=true` recoveries; blurred
toast, animated loaders and layout drawn on every new device at the same and
at changed sizes; input works; no panic, crash record or D3D11 debug-layer
error ([evidence](https://github.com/dykamino-coder/gpui-kamin-ide-priv-evidence/tree/c8d86808da08385ab438972e325b27a4ad8420c2/incidents/INC-2026-0023/evidence/2026-10-11-runtime-acceptance-22fb1ad)).

Not covered yet: sleep/resume and RDP transitions, DPI change, failed
recovery / allocation failure, and live-object counts for the release of old
resources. The incident stays in `verify` until those parts of the gate run.
