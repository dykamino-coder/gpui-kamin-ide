## Symptom

Any change on screen — a blinking caret, a spinner, a hover state — makes
KaminIDE repaint the entire window. With a GPU this is cheap; on the software
rasterizer (WARP: RDP sessions, VMs without a GPU) every frame costs CPU in
proportion to the whole window area (INC-2026-0001 measured ~12 Mpx painted per
frame for a 1.8 Mpx window before #261).

## Confirmed facts (main `b1ff5c2`, 2026-10-15)

- GPUI caches unchanged views on the CPU side (`reuse_prepaint`/`reuse_paint`
  in `vendor/gpui/src/window.rs`): unchanged views are not rebuilt.
- The scene keeps a `BoundsTree` for primitive ordering and hit testing, but
  there is no damage region: all primitives of the scene are submitted to the
  GPU every frame.
- The DirectX renderer (`vendor/gpui-windows/src/directx_renderer.rs`) clears
  and redraws the full frame: `ScissorEnable: false`, no `Present1` with dirty
  rectangles, and the swapchain presents the whole back buffer.
- The built-in HTML engine (`crates/html/src/render/mod.rs`, `render(nodes,
  opts)`) rebuilds the cascade, layout and GPUI elements of the whole page for
  any change; there is no style or layout invalidation by subtree. This is
  tracked here as context; the engine pipeline is a separate follow-up.

## Expected direction (to be confirmed by the design)

1. Compute a damage region per frame from what changed between scenes
   (views that were rebuilt, moved or re-composited; CEF texture updates).
2. Redraw only primitives intersecting the damage, with a scissor per damage
   rectangle, preserving the rest of the previous frame (a swapchain mode that
   keeps the back buffer contents, or an explicit copy).
3. Present with dirty rectangles (`IDXGISwapChain1::Present1`).
4. Fall back to a full repaint on resize, device loss and DPI change.

### Measurement gap
In the `[gpu]` diag line, `kpx` is the area of the submitted primitives (`batch_area`), not of the scissored part. `frames=` counts only frames that were drawn (`Keep` frames are not counted). So the acceptance criterion "painted area from `[gpu]`" cannot be read on 1.0.111. The available measures are:
- `frames=` (how many frames are not `Keep`);
- `frame_gpu_us` with `KAMIN_DIAG_GPU_TIMING=1`, compared against `GPUI_DAMAGE_REDRAW=0`.

Passive read of `diag.log` from a running 1.0.111 on the default (hardware) adapter, 2560×1380, idle, nothing driven:
- 2–3 frames per second are drawn rather than kept; the `[dirty]` source is `notify@src/web/pump.rs:46`;
- per drawn frame, submitted area is quads 21597 kpx plus polychrome sprites 1936 kpx.

Whether these frames were drawn within a rectangle or whole cannot be read from these lines (see the measurement gap above).

### Remainder
1. **Counter of the drawn area (blocks acceptance).** Add the following to the `[gpu]` line:
   - the plan of each frame: keep, full or within;
   - the area of the scissor rectangle per drawn frame.

   Without this counter the card's acceptance cannot be measured.
2. **Acceptance measurement** on a release with the counter, on WARP (`KAMIN_GPU_ADAPTER=software`) and the hardware adapter:
   - cases: caret blink, a spinner, hover;
   - read the drawn area per frame and `frame_gpu_us` (`KAMIN_DIAG_GPU_TIMING=1`) against `GPUI_DAMAGE_REDRAW=0`;
   - drive the app by commands only, with no screen capture.
3. **`Present1` dirty rects: decide by number.** Item 2 gives the drawn area per frame.
   - `Present` without dirty rects lets DWM and the RDP encoder treat the whole back buffer as changed.
   - The window area divided by the damaged area is the upper bound of what `Present1` can save there.
   - Implement it only if that ratio is large for the item 2 cases and an RDP session confirms it (CPU of DWM and of the RDP encoder). On a local hardware adapter no gain is expected.
4. **External textures force a full frame.** Check whether the shell's idle frames carry an external texture (CEF surfaces). If they do, the design's CEF sub-rectangle damage (3.1) is the next change.
5. Up to four damage rectangles (3.3) and skipping instances outside the damage: only if item 2 shows the single union rectangle or the submission cost matters.
