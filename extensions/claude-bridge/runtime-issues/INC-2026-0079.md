## Confirmed facts and limits

At `dbf157479dfe53e75664d0358bab2fc6a4c3e8d2`, the console sets `scrollback: 0`,
hides both viewport and custom scrollbars, and strips application mouse mode
sequences from live output and restored snapshots. Headless probes reproduce
absent normal-screen history and discarded alternate-screen mouse modes. These
source observations do not attest native wheel delivery or a live CLI session.

Commit `916cdd2ab6b658add61ab12da43158136514fe2d` (#300) intentionally removed
history and scrollbar after stale TUI frames and scrollbar width interfered with
panel fitting. Its fit-to-panel correction remains required; its absolute
"never scrolls" constraint is explicitly superseded by this incident.

## Required outcome and runtime gate

Normal screen has bounded scrollback and a visible working scrollbar; wheel and
dragging the thumb reach older lines. The panel itself remains fitted without
outer overflow when resized. In alternate screen, preserve application-declared
mouse modes and forward wheel input using its protocol. Text selection uses an
explicit modifier. Do not globally translate wheel events to PageUp/PageDown or
add scrolling tips as a substitute for the fix.

On the exact proposed Windows candidate, lead opens the Bridge console, emits
more numbered normal-screen lines than its viewport, scrolls by wheel and thumb,
checks top/bottom and retained-history bounds, then resizes the panel and window.
Run the supported full-screen CLI in alternate screen with its declared mouse
modes: wheel reaches application history, ordinary mouse gestures follow its
protocol, modifier-drag selects text, copy/paste and keyboard input still work.
Check hide/show, reconnect/snapshot restore, adjacent toolbar hover/click/focus,
terminal focus and visual scrollbar/panel fitting. Record candidate SHA, CLI and
OS versions, actions and results. Automated/headless checks do not replace this
gate. A CLI that does not advertise mouse handling cannot establish that case.
