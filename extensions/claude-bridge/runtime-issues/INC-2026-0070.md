## Reported symptom and limits

The owner reports two failures of one Chat tab on 2026-10-19/20 with Bridge
6.3.152: after the `open-claude-bridge` container was recreated, the tab opened
a new empty conversation instead of resuming its prior conversation. The old
transcript remained intact. One failure followed an active turn with teammates;
the other followed a hang, with up to eight teammates previously running.
The same tab resumed successfully after other recreations that day. The owner's
estimate of roughly one failure per hundred recreations is not a measured rate.

Use **conversation A** for the intended transcript and **conversation B** for
the newly adopted conversation. This is loss of the tab's association, not
proven deletion or irreversible loss of A; manual recovery may remain possible.
The reported stale/missing client transcript mirror is a separate observation,
not proof that the tab lacked A's ID.

The brief reports about **1,245 detaches in roughly an hour**, 1,135 reattaches,
161 creates and 51 detach-grace destructions in the affected run. These are
owner-extracted aggregates, not independently verified telemetry. No per-tab
protocol trace, raw lifecycle journal or raw transcript accompanies this intake.

INC-0043 concerns incomplete history after an IDE restart; INC-0054 concerns
missing/expired transcripts and recovery. Here the owner reports an intact old
transcript with a newly adopted identity, so neither card establishes this cause.

## Verified source and corrections to the brief

Audited fresh `origin/main`: `f441fdc2a9218dce5da4bb1922db82f9ea3b3960`,
2026-10-20. This is newer than the reported deployment. The links below resolve
inside the repository; line numbers refer to that audited revision.

1. [Client startSession](../extension/src/main/ws/connection-manager.ts#L419-L478)
   sends `session:resume` with wire field `conversationId` when its stored ID is
   truthy, otherwise `session:create`. `resumeConversationId` is the internal
   Bridge configuration field, not the wire field name.
2. [session-ws.ts](../server/src/core/pty/session-ws.ts#L192-L230) calls
   `createSession` without a resume ID for `session:create`.
   [session:resume](../server/src/core/pty/session-ws.ts#L350-L480) first looks up
   a live conversation scoped to the authenticated token, then passes
   `msg.conversationId` as `resumeConversationId` for a cold start. The brief's
   assertion that current warm reattach needs only a Bridge session ID is
   **not supported**: [findSessionByConversation](../server/src/core/pty/session-core.ts#L760-L768)
   matches the conversation ID or a compact alias. Live reattach replays history;
   default detach grace is ten minutes. Container recreation loses the in-memory
   live-session lookup.
3. [session-core.ts](../server/src/core/pty/session-core.ts#L141-L210) follows
   compact links. With a resume ID it calls `findOrRecreateSettingsDir`; without
   one (and without `reuseSettingsDir`) it creates a fresh unique directory.
   Failed lookup separately clears the resume ID and sets `resumeNotFound`.
   Chain-tip resolution can legitimately change an ID; distinguish it from
   accidental adoption.
4. [Resume helpers](../server/src/core/pty/session-resume-helpers.ts#L33-L114)
   search project transcripts, repair/recreate the settings directory and try
   archive recovery. An intact reported file makes recovery plausible; it does
   not prove lookup would have succeeded in the incident's runtime environment
   or that ownership was checked there.
5. [Watcher wiring](../server/src/core/pty/session-core.ts#L503-L535) publishes a
   discovered ID through `session:conversation-id`.
   [Client dispatcher](../extension/src/main/ws/handle-server-message.ts#L381-L392)
   calls `setConversationId` and notifies the tab;
   [setConversationId](../extension/src/main/ws/connection-manager.ts#L970-L984)
   updates connection identity/mirror; [core-ipc.ts](../extension/src/core-ipc.ts#L229-L238)
   assigns the tab's conversation ID and saves session state, while
   [sessions-bridge.ts](../extension/src/sessions-bridge.ts#L45-L57) persists it
   into the owning IDE session's metadata. This verifies a possible fresh
   session → new conversation ID → persisted tab ID chain, not its occurrence here.
6. [Lifecycle logger](../server/src/core/logging/lifecycle.ts#L69-L103) allowlists
   metadata and uses a per-boot HMAC of the Bridge session ID. Current events
   omit requested conversation, selected directory and old/new conversation
   transitions. Aggregate counts therefore cannot identify the affected tab or
   establish its request.

## Hypotheses, not conclusions

- The client sent no resume ID, or had already adopted B during the preceding
  storm. Why A's ID was missing remains unknown. Persistence, stale tab state
  and timing around a hung/failed turn are candidates.
- Current client code explicitly drops the ID after three establishment failures
  ([armEstablishTimer](../extension/src/main/ws/connection-manager.ts#L464-L478))
  or repeated rapid resumed CLI exits
  ([noteSessionExit](../extension/src/main/ws/connection-manager.ts#L490-L510)).
  These are verified fallback mechanisms, not a diagnosis of the historical client.
- Absence of the fresh-session warning in the owner's `docker logs` review does
  **not** prove absence of a resume request: completeness, versions and request
  correlation were not captured. That warning belongs to failed lookup, not
  every fresh-session creation.
- [INC-0069](INC-2026-0069.md) confirms the retired-subagent replay loop; source
  fix [#380](https://github.com/dykamino-coder/gpui-kamin-ide/pull/380) is merged.
  [scanSubagents](../server/src/core/pty/jsonl-watcher.ts#L313-L330) watches the
  conversation's `subagents/` folder; the brief reports teammate transcripts in
  that same folder. Re-sending them could amplify load with up to eight teammates
  and plausibly drive the storm. This is a **hypothesis**: no correlated buffer,
  event-loop, memory or CPU measurements attribute the 1,245 detaches to that
  loop. Its fix does not establish that INC-0070 is fixed.

## Side-issue verification

| Claim | Verified scope and remaining uncertainty |
| --- | --- |
| Freshest JSONL adoption | [scanDirectory / scanForJsonl](../server/src/core/pty/jsonl-watcher.ts#L548-L616) select the eligible file with greatest mtime. Eligibility is after watcher start, or after zero for resume as configured by session-core. Another eligible JSONL in that directory can therefore be selected. Wrong-file adoption here is unverified; this differs from compact-chain tip resolution. |
| All-project scan with null slugDir | The constructor permits absent settingsDir and then null slugDir; scanForJsonl scans all project directories for the freshest result. A repository-wide production call-site search finds only session-core's constructor call, always passed a nonempty settingsDir from its exhaustive branches. No reachable current production path to null slugDir was found. Cross-user attachment is a latent conditional risk, **not confirmed production disclosure**. If reachability is established, classify it **P1 security/privacy** and contain that boundary. |
| reverseSlugToPath replaces dashes | [reverseSlugToPath](../server/src/core/pty/session-resume-helpers.ts#L122-L142) replaces dashes with underscores in the token-directory portion of the UUID-tail case, preserving the session UUID. The non-UUID-tail case differs. Lossy reconstruction is source-confirmed; reported duplicate directories and the claim of no resume effect are unverified. |
| Anonymous lifecycle volume | [Dockerfile](../server/Dockerfile#L95) declares the lifecycle volume target; a bare run without an explicit mount can receive an anonymous volume. [Checked-in Compose](../server/docker-compose.yml#L24-L29) already mounts a named lifecycle volume. The affected deployment's anonymous-volume history is owner-reported, not independently inspected. Recreation need not delete old volumes, but can detach their journals from the new container. |

## Next research artifact

Add bounded instrumentation in a separate research PR covering **both Bridge
and client**, with allowlisting, retention and identity-correlation tests:

- Bridge create/reattach: requested resume-ID presence and correlated identity,
  `resumeNotFound`, selected settings-directory identity and selection reason;
  every `session:conversation-id`: previous/new identity and provenance (fresh,
  resume, compact-chain, watcher discovery). Include connection/request/boot
  correlation, detach/reattach/exit timing and replay load.
- Client: ID held by the tab before reconnect; outgoing create/resume type and
  requested identity; every overwrite/persistence callback with previous/new
  identity and reason; watchdog counters, exit codes and timing. Distinguish
  mirror state from tab/connection state. Capture before recreation too, to
  detect earlier adoption during the storm.
- Preserve the lifecycle privacy boundary: explicit allowlisted pseudonymous
  correlation in durable metadata; any necessary raw ID/directory mapping only
  in a reviewed private capture. The logger silently ignores unallowlisted
  fields, so adding fields only at call sites is insufficient. Correlate across
  boots and client without publishing identifiers or credentials.
- Deployment owner verifies an explicit persistent lifecycle mount; do not
  propose a redundant Compose fix. Measure CPU/memory/event-loop/socket
  backpressure and teammate replays before proposing concurrency limits.
- Controlled disposable cases: long conversation with active teammates,
  completed/failed turn, forced container recreation, idle control, warm reconnect,
  repeated establish failures and rapid exits. Registration executes no evidence
  commands and does not mutate production.

Locate the earliest divergence or report the precise missing trace and owner.
A future recovery decision could preserve A and offer an explicit choice before
adopting B, as suggested in the brief; this is an unaccepted product proposal.
